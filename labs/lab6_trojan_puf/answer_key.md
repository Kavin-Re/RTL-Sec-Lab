# Lab 6 answer key

1. The threshold fixes the false-alarm rate of the whole run (the chance that a clean device alarms in some window). Tuning it on the trojan runs makes the detection number look better but the real false-alarm rate is unknown and typically much higher; calibrate on clean simulations (here: 5 % of clean runs of 300 windows alarm at h = 40), then measure power on separate trojan runs with other seeds.
2. Examples: a trojan whose effect keeps the channel distribution uniform (for instance a delayed or time-shifted but uniform sequence, or one that leaks information over another path), a bias below the detectable level (2.3 % is detected in only about 20 % of 2000-hop windows), and one that is active only rarely. Extra checks: golden-reference comparison on one side of the link (the receiver regenerates the expected hops from the key and sees the first mismatch), long-window or sequential tests, power or timing comparison, supply-chain controls. This detector cannot find a trojan before its trigger fires because the output is bit-identical until then.
3. The PUF bit error rate is about 1.6 % nominal and about 5 % at +60 C. BCH(15,7,t=2) corrects at most 2 errors per 15-bit block, which at 5 % is exceeded often enough that only about half of the devices regenerate the key at +60 C. Repetition x3 first lowers the error rate seen by BCH, at the price of 3x the response bits and area. Fuzzy-extractor secrets must also be hashed and the helper data is public (its leakage is not analysed in this kit).

Common mistakes: 13 instead of 12 degrees of freedom; calibrating on the trojan runs; mixing up the single-window and CUSUM thresholds; judging uniqueness without removing the layout gradient; treating the PUF model as evidence about real silicon.
